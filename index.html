About the Digital Memory Drum

The Memory Drum is a classic piece of experimental psychology laboratory equipment, originally invented by Georg Elias Müller and Friedrich Schumann in 1887, to study serial learning, rote memory, and nonsense syllable retention. This web-based digital simulator modernizes the traditional mechanical apparatus. If you missed your psychology lab practical, this free digital simulator allows you to learn how to use a memory drum online and recreate strict serial learning and paired-associate trials from home.

In Dedication

This digital simulator is dedicated to Dr. Mary Whiton Calkins (1863–1930), the pioneering American psychologist who originally developed the paired-associate learning technique—one of the foundational cognitive methods tested using the mechanical memory drum. Despite fulfilling every requirement for a Ph.D. with distinction, Harvard University consistently refused to grant her the degree solely on the basis of her gender. Her legacy is a lasting reminder that brilliant, necessary work exists entirely independent of institutional validation.
